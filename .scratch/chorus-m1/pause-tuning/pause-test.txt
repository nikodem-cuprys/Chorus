The Pause Test

by Chorus



CHAPTER I.

The kettle had been singing for some time before anyone thought to lift it off the stove. Martha poured the water, set out two cups, and sat down by the window to wait.

"He said he would be here by four," she said. "It is nearly five."

"Then he will be here by six," said her brother, without looking up from his book. "He always is."

She laughed at that, because it was true, and because there was nothing else to do. Outside, the rain had started again.

To pass the time, her brother read aloud from the book on his knee:

    Twinkle, twinkle, little bat!
    How I wonder what you're at!
    Up above the world you fly,
    Like a tea-tray in the sky.

"That is not how it goes," said Martha.

"It is how it goes in this book," he said.

* * *

By the time the knock came, the tea was cold and the lamps were lit. Martha opened the door, and there he stood, soaked through and smiling, as if nothing in the world were the matter.



CHAPTER II.

The next morning was bright and still. Nobody spoke of the rain, or of the cold tea, or of the hour.

    How doth the little crocodile
    Improve his shining tail,
    And pour the waters of the Nile
    On every golden scale!

"You have been reading that book again," said Martha.

"I have," said her brother. "It improves with every reading."
